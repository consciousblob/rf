From a ChatGPT Q:

**What is the primary bottleneck preventing humanity from functioning like an effective collective intelligence?**
One of the main bottlenecks is current technology, or rather, lack of it. There is latent knowledge (a lot of which could form solutions to the problems we face) "in between" minds - that is, in the spaces where thoughts are (1) not externalised and (2) not synthesised. For example, my conception of mindscaping covers both individual thought spaces, and a collectivisation of these. Imagining we only cultivated the individual thought spaces... this alone, I think, would unlock much of the latent knowledge, which is latent because we don't take the time to explicitise it, to pull it out of our brains, and then develop it. Since I view each mind as a unique processor, I think a core priority should be encouraging all minds to *engage* - to process what they feel inclined to, to really devote mental energy in grappling with whatever problems/questions they feel pulled toward. Then, if they share the products of this processing (output), other minds can input and process, and this collectivisation can unlock the remaining latent knowledge, the extent of which will increase as we enhance the mindware for developing and synthesising thought. I feel that if this technology were to exist - where it's simple, easy, and would seem strange *not to* develop an independent thoughtspace (mindscape, digital garden), then it could pull people from other "distractions" and less meaningful uses of time, and make it easier to dedicate more time to independent thought, which is better for all of us. This technology can replace some of the dominant time-wasters of our time - e.g. endless, pointless media/news consumption, social media scrolling, mindless (over)watching of TV, etc. And it can be a snowballing positive feedback loop, where the more people spend more time cultivating their own mindscape, the more evidently useful and rewarding it becomes, and the more people want to spend more time doing this. And then more people will be developing mindware, and we'll advance our abilities to share these thoughts, independently and collectively. And the kinds of technologies that fuel divisions and hostilities will dissipate, become obsolete, fostering cooperation and collaboration over conflict and ego-compelled competition.

However, this digital infrastructure must be developed in tandem with *cognitive* or mental infrastructure. We need to develop our ability to use these tools effectively - for example, how to think deeply and critically and collaboratively, how to maintain an open mind, etc. The two will evolve together, hand in hand. And this would be helped along with a particular emphasis on early years, and introducing this (digital and cognitive) infrastructure from a young age. The technology can become a kind of companion, an enabler, of humans' highest, best, most "noble" brain traits and capabilities, from childhood.
