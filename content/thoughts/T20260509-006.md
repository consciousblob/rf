title: Discussing 'better' in terms of healthcare via genomics
---

This is a core aspect of my life philosophy and [[my-worldview]]  - the perpetual pursuit of "better", however we define it (and *better* converging on our ideas of "better"). We can prepend "better" to basically anything (e.g. life, work, economy, relationships, society, world, etc.) and then, use this as a shelter/umbrella under which to begin aligning our views and converging on shared conception of meaning/value/importance, and ways to honour/realise that. For example, we all want [[better-healthcare]]. But what does that actually look like? We need to decide what "better" means, within a particular spatial context (local/national/global), how we'd recognise/measure it, and then, agree on the best (or, better) ways to actually achieve it. So, say it includes "more genetically-tailored interventions". Then, maybe a way to recognise this is "(1) we are now able to offer a genetic/genomic profile to anyone who opts in, and (2) the number of interventions which diverge from what would have normally been recommended based on generally inferred statistical evidence has increased (and this increase is statistically significant) and (3) outcomes from these interventions are deemed more positive". Then, we might agree on the need to focus R&D efforts on improving [[genomics]] technology/capabilities until we can meet this recognition criteria.
