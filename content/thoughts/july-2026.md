### [[Sun 19-07-2026]]

**Piano songs** [[+musical-expression]]
I've been playing around on the piano and put together some notes I like the sound of, forming a kind of nebula that will hopefully collapse into a song as I try new things and try to string the segments together. I've never "properly" written a song before - I can only ever seem to manage *very short* fragments, but have difficulty combining these into a coherent, decent-sounding final piece. I also have difficulty finishing a song - in my random key-pressing during the (attempted) development process, I end up finding other sequences/segments/combinations I like the sound of, but that don't really fit the current thing I'm working on, and so I get side-tracked with the new sound, and then I get overwhelmed by the number of different fragments that would probably only fit / correspond with different songs, and I want to record/capture them all so I don't forget, but don't know how, so I try to memorise but because they're all pretty different, I get them muddled, and then it all becomes a big flop / f(l)ailure and I then resort to just playing the ~3.5 songs in my repertoire that I haven't expanded for almost a decade to give my ego some sense of accomplishment. Anyway, I'm saying all this because I've come to a decision/conclusion that I no longer want to approach piano-ing like this. For one, I actually want to learn new songs (and complete the ones I haven't). But primarily, I want to actually materialise at least one *full song*. I want to develop my ability to take a small seed (string of a few notes) and nourish it into something that resembles an actual "piece of music". I have felt the pure joy of witnessing even the tiny, barely-existent beginnings of a musical representation of emotion/thought and I just know that it's something I don't think I could forgive myself for if I didn't devote energy to "taking it all the way" into a song, and then ideally, hopefully, a collection of songs/music blobs, each acting as a "spokesperson" for different clusters in TB's internal space of emotional experience. I also want to perhaps add/layer lyrics/vocals with this as well, but I think for now, my priority should be the piano "skeleton"/basis. I'm curious about how experiences, masterful songwriters approach the process - both the differences and commonalities in their approaches. I guess that some start with lyrics, the other stuff (what to call it? not "music" because lyrics are encompassed by that - just non-lyrics? non-lyrical music? "instrumentals"?) later, or vice versa. I feel drawn to both approaches: sometimes lyrics occur in TM, and then it's a matter of matching some kind of instrumental thing to these, but other times, just fiddling around with the keys, random progressions, chords, yields something that sounds like "song potential" and then lyrics could be considered later, an optional add-on. But I feel it's probably best, as in "most productive" to take one approach for now and stick to it, and then maybe trial the other after/later. Currently, I'm leaning towards the instrumental-first-lyrics-later approach.

I want to (1) learn about general song development process, including musicians match lyrics to instrumental, (2) best way to record segments of song (along with potential lyrics) during development. So, for (2), I want to be able to easily record which notes I pressed (MIDI) and as part of this, see the notes and corresponding keys for easier recreation (minimise friction in the re-learning process if I forget certain parts), and manipulate the notes digitally (without e.g. re-recording the whole thing), which I guess is a defining characteristic of MIDI. But then, eventually, once a song is finished, I want to be able to record the actual sound emanating from the piano, not just the digital note representations, which will require e.g. different cable, connection. But for now, I only care about the MIDI (but will keep the sound-recording in mind, in case any equipment I might need could "kill both birds with one stone" (I hate everything about that phrase apart from its utility in conveying the meaning I mean (yes I know it's "two" birds not "both" but TM adapted for semantic fit))) #whatev I guess then what I need is (1) hardware (probably including a MIDI cable able to connect via USB to laptop) and (2) software - some sort of program, ideally free + open-source, to record, see & play with the notes.

^Using the #whatev tag to more clearly demarcate the end of a particularly "astray" rambling.

[[mindscaping]]
**Mindscaping** refers to the creation and usage of infrastructure for better (intra + inter) thinking.
Mindscaping involves the development of **mindware**: the hardware and software that can "host" our minds - store and manipulate thought.
We can consider 2 categories of mindware: (1) intrathinking and (2) interthinking. Of course, there will be overlap between the two, but for now, given the quite primitive state of existing mindware, it's useful to think in terms of these containers.

Each mind/individual has their own **mindscape** - their "independent/individual thought space" (ITS). These mindscapes can be connected to form the **Mindscape** (the aggregation/integration of all mindscapes - the "collective thought space" (CTS)), using mindware.

One type of mindware could be a "syndicator": all minds output thought in their own ITS, and then the syndicator distributes these thoughts in different formats across different platforms. This syndicator thing is possibly only relevant/necessary during the transition to a digital-cognitive infrastructure in which all minds have their own ITS, which interact via mindware (as opposed to thoughts being shared via monolithic privately owned platforms). 

Another type of mindware could be an "aggregator", which can refer to different but similar functionalities. One is the aggregation of minds - so, an aggregator mindware module could identify likeminds (or even minds with just a likeness in a certain realm - say, a particular area of interest) and "bring them together" (togather?) and then provide a space for them to interact and collaborate on the identified area of likeness. For example, minds wanting to execute a similar kind of project (e.g. a community kitchen, a particular avenue of research in nuclear fusion, a hypothesis about the cause of a certain disease, etc.) can share thoughts related to this project on their ITS before it has taken form; an aggregator comes along and recognises that this potential project is similar to one proposed/discussed by another few minds; it notifies these minds and invites them to join a shared thought space (a hub); the minds can accept/decline the invitation, and if accepted, their thoughts related to the project (which they can explicitly indicate/tag, or can be inferred by the aggregator) can be automatically displayed on the project hub, and the hub can also hold a space for direct discussion. **Direct digital discussion** could be better enabled by e.g. automatically recorded meetings that take place on the hub (in the context of its project) which each come with an accompanying collaborative whiteboard (like Miro, and this type of thing is another object/functionality mindware could replicate/pertain to) to enable multi-media expression of thought, and real-time capture of this expression, for future reference and re-input.

**What is TM?**
