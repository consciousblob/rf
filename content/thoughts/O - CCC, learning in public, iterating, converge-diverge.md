I've started to realise that I find it far more fun and rewarding to "knuckle down" and get lost in the weeds of learning something new, unfamiliar, challenging, when I can (1) see all my thoughts/learnings in front of me, unfolding, developing and (2) share these, and know that a representation of my learning, and the effort/energy I'm pouring into it, is available for anyone to see. It's about creating some kind of concrete, visible, physical "evidence" of the learning, as I go along.

I think showing *how* we're learning something - the process, journey - is just as, or perhaps even more, valuable than showing the results, products, destination, in all its messy, silly, chaotic glory.

I want to get comfortable with showing my misconceptions. I want to be able to e.g. release an incomplete exposition / representation of my understanding of something, with many mistakes, inaccuracies, imperfections, flaws, and get over myself and my egoic desire to be seen as perfectly correct, right, intelligent, competent, capable, 100% of the time.

So, here is an experiment I want to initiate: when I'm learning something, I want to create a running log of my understanding of that thing. It has to be/have a low barrier/threshold of quality to release (I'm all about lowering that activation energy). I'll have certain strands I'm pursuing, wanting to develop my understanding of. But, I might not consistently pursue them (might e.g. start a scope, and then not continue it, move onto something else, return much later down the timeline).
