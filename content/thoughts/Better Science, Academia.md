- Divergence: you write up your findings in whatever way you feel most effectively communicates them.
	- You are encouraged to communicate your research as creatively as possible, exploring different ways of representing what you've found, your current understanding, and allowing your unique voice, approach and worldview to shine through.
	- An AI tool follows you throughout the process, noting what sources inform your research - this is manually mediated by you, in that when you visit a node/note on the mindscape you can choose to include it in your "input stream" - a record of everything you've input - and e.g. explicitly tag it with a particular research endeavour, so that it automatically forms this string of all sources used in that endeavour and can compile a more traditional "reference list" from this.
	- Additionally, minds can choose that when any other mind inputs its outputs, they are automatically added to their input stream, to e.g. prevent plagiarism - and this could be a feature of certain nodes - that in order to access the outputs of that node, you must consent to any outputs you visit being added to your input stream (regardless of how long you spent inputting/engaging with it) - but, you can e.g. add notes/comments/properties to each input on your stream to indicate how much you engaged with it and note whether it was e.g. an accidental click or you had a brief look but realised it wasn't what you were looking for, etc.
	- so basically, this immutable record of inputs being written as condition on you accessing node outputs
- Convergence: various AI tools perform standardisation on your work, according to different briefs of different hubs (like journals) [[MW - immutable chronological record of inputs]]
	- Converting to the standard format of Abstract, Intro, Methods, Results, Conclusion, etc.
