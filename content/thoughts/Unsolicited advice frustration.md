I want to take a moment to rant about something that's been really bothering me lately: unsolicited advice. And not only advice, but actual disapproval, and feeling the need to defend/justify how I spend my time.

For context, a lot of my family push back on "how much work I do". They say I work/study too much - that I spend too much time in my room - that I need more "leisure" time. Particularly, I feel my mum getting quite annoyed/angry with how much time I spend in my room - she points out that I used to spend more time downstairs, and asks why I spend more time up in my room now. There are many reasons, but primarily, I have always been like this - it was only after the psychosis that I would spend lots of time *not* in my room, because I became a shell of myself and incredibly dependent on others, unable to exist independently, and hated spending time alone. I now see it as a blessing that I've returned to my "old" introverted self (improved version) because it means I'm able to focus more on my passions, and that I actually HAVE passions (which I didn't for a long while after the psychosis). I think my mum got used to my more dependent state and seems to have forgotten that there's nothing unprecedented about this - I've always liked time to myself. Additionally, whenever I try to share my interests with her in more depth, she shuts me down, explicitly expressing that she's not interested. So, I literally can't explore my passions around her, because she refuses to discuss them. And like most people in my life, because my passions are so "pie in the sky" and I'm so "intense" about them (many people have either said this or given the impression they think this), I don't feel comfortable discussing/exploring them with them. I also don't know of anyone in my social vicinity who has the same interests with the same intensity, and so I "retreat to my room", to my own mind, where I *can* fully explore and nurture these passions/interests, without feeling (mis)judged and "weird" and "something wrong with me" for entertaining them.

Then, for example, my mum frequently complains of how she's stressed, struggling to find time to do things she needs to, etc. So, when she's telling me / implying that I should work *less*, why should I listen? I feel I'm managing my time excellently right now; I am perfectly happy, satisfied, fulfilled and stress-free. But for the people who tell me how I should better be spending my time / not spending my time, I do not think this is the case.

Then, I feel many people have this misconception that I spend literally all of my free time studying. This is certainly not the case. I would say I spend *at best* 50% of my free time studying. The rest of it is what I would call my work *and* leisure time. My *life work* and stuff I just enjoy. For example, creating the root-finding site, writing, developing mindware, learning about neural nets, AI, different economic systems, the fediverse, watching YouTube, reading, drawing, researching, dancing, working out, etc. There is such a *variety* of activities I get up to "in my room". I take care of all my responsibilities. I study a decent-ish amount (though arguably could do with more). I socialise regularly and keep in touch with friends. I make time to decompress when I need to. I exercise and look after my health. Overall, I function well. So I do not understand why people keep telling me how I'm living incorrectly, according to their assumptions of what I even do. That's it - they *assume* they know how I spend my time - that I'm constantly revising like some academic robot. That is just not the case. I try time and time again to explain this, and they don't listen. They just see me doing the odd assignment/reading a textbook and assume that's how I spend all my time. Then, it makes me not want to do any "work" in front of them, because I know this will only solidify this assumption, and so it thickens the filter I put on myself and how I show up around them. Maybe it's also partially my fault because as an "excuse" to reclaim some time by e.g. opting out of an event/gathering (which is not a constant thing - I still make time for lots of events/gatherings), or in response to when they ask what I've been up to, I'll say "doing work" or something. They take that to mean uni work, but I actually mean *life work* that pertains to all my interests (that I treat with the same seriousness as my uni work), but I don't mention any of this, because I don't trust them to understand.

Recently, my Nana said she wants to talk to me about how much time I'm spending studying, and that I need to make time for leisure too. I do know that it comes from a good place, but it's incredibly frustrating, because she actually has no idea how I spend my time. Like, yes, I am studious, but I'm also naturally intelligent and so it can often look like I've put more work into something than I actually have. I know that sounds arrogant, but the arrogant tone comes out when I feel this exasperated with constantly having to explain myself and explain why my autonomous decisions are valid. Also, *so what* if I choose to study all the time? I mean, as long as I'm balancing that with everything else (health, socialising, general responsibilities), ultimately, it's *my business* if that's how I choose to spend my time. When I do choose to study a lot, it doesn't come from a place of stress, but from a place curiosity and intrinsic enjoyment and love of learning. If I enjoy it, what's the problem? My "work" *is* my leisure activity.

Also, I want to mention the idea of ideal lives, and differing personal definitions of success. The thing is, *no one* around me, in my social vicinity, has a life I would consider "my ideal life". *Not one* of the individuals offering their opinions on how I spend my time have achieved any of the things I want to achieve in life. Now, this isn't to devalue/belittle their achievements - they are amazing in their own rights, and I admire them. But they are not *my* things. I have my own thing in life, my own goals, my own path. And *no one* around me has ever walked that path, or done anything remotely similar to what I want to do. So, how can I expect to be able to achieve the things I want, by copy-pasting *their* approaches to life, and following their advice? Like, their definition of "work-life balance", leisure time, success, etc. completely diverges from mine, and that's *fine* - but then, don't expect me to take your advice seriously if you haven't achieved the things I'm aiming for.

Why do people feel entitled to tell me how I should be living my life / spending my time? I think that because they believe they have my best interests and are older and supposedly wiser than me, they feel this entitled to offer unsolicited advice. I know that it most often comes from a place of care and wanting the best for me, but it's incredibly frustrating to keep expending energy justifying why I spend time the way I do. I don't try to tell others how they should be spending their lives, so I feel I deserve the same respect for my autonomy. I'm not harming anyone, including myself. I'm happy and healthy. So please, why can't you let me just exist how I want to?

I watched a Teal Swan video on recognising excellence which I found really resonant and probably relevant here.
