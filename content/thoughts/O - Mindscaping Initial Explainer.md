title: O - Mindscaping Initial Explainer

- AI doesn't capture nuanced, creative explanations
	- Current GenAI presents a rather homogenised/averaged/generalised representation of knowledge in response to a prompt. It smooths over any quirks in natural human output styles (explanations, elucidations, discussions, representations, answers, explorations).
	- Humans have different ways of processing (and inputting/outputting), different preferences, tendencies. Every object can be represented in (I would argue) an infinite number of ways, from infinitely many angles, perspectives.
	- And while AI captures many of these, a large subset, it (1) doesn't capture all and (2) averages them out, so they lose their "character". This is akin to applying a one-size-fits-all approach to education and learning.
	- Indeed, there is some degree of customisability and personalisability in its explanations, but overall, between the main different systems, I have found them to be fairly similar (to each other, and in how they explain different things).
	- I think we need to reintroduce the human element - the little quirky random representations, analogies, comparisons. The way that a teacher tries to explain something, and it's just not clicking, and they keep experiementing with different approaches, perspectives, until finally, *ohhh, I get it now!*
	- This, I believe, is not primarily a function of optimising explanation, finding the ultimate best, optimal explanation, but rather of having a whole jungle of different explanations available, and searching for ones that click (and finding like-minds that consistently or frequently seem to explain things in a way that gels/meshes with your mind, way of thinking).
	- We can each craft our own different explainers, (de)tours, investigations, representations, maps, analogies, etc. - can be as long or short as the mind sees fit - could do multiple versions, long and short and anything in between - and release them into the wild for other minds to discover.
	- We can have mindware so that if a thought shared is particularly helpful, resonant or effective at giving a mind clarity, they can upvote it or tag it with relevant objects it helped the mind come to terms with, so other minds are more likely to encounter it.
	- I just find that when I'm learning something and come to a point of clarity (after an onslaught of confusion), I want to be able to share it, without weaving it into some grand exposition about an entire subject or sub-subject. Also, when I come back to it a while later, I've lost some of the clarity associated with the initial discovery/uncovery (and recovery of sanity) and have to retrace my steps. So, I need to more verbosely walk through exactly how I arrived at that clarity, for my own sake, but if I'm doing that privately, why not publicise in case it's useful to anyone else? I personally would find 20 explanations of a concept from micro-experts (non-experts, but that have become slight experts in that very specific thing they worked through) more helpful than 3 explanations from experts.
	- The core thing is that in sharing these explanations, we're not claiming to be an expert and even to be correct - we might be wrong. But, the best explanations will rise to the top, and if they aren't completely correct, their visibility will expose them to the minds that know more about the objects they pertain to who can offer the constructive criticism to correct them.
