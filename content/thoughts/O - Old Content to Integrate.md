# Explainer
- Small edits to correct blatant mistakes if I notice them (e.g. misspellings, repeated words), but other than this, I've left the writing unaltered. So, it's pretty messy - both within sentences (grammar, punctuation, sentence structure), and between them (overall structure, section ordering, etc.)
- I tended to use \[square brackets] to indicate different ways of representing the thing I'm talking about*, so I could decide later in the editing phase which representation to go with for the final piece. But since this stuff was written quite a few years ago, I think it makes more sense to just show the raw stuff, and save my editing energies for newer thought pieces. I do however acknowledge that the abundance of these square bracket expansions can make the writing more jarring to process, so apologies 🙏
	- Actually, in current writings, I tend to do a similar thing, but via excessive usage of slash/stroke instead of square brackets.
- classification framework
	- I was trying to classify things using a framework that distinguishes along 2 axes: micro/macro, and social/natural
- MHoN
	- quite integral to worldview back then - still kind of, but more subconsciously (less overtly)
- Socialism, Marxism
	- I went through an era of identifying as a socialist/communist/Marxist (my use of slashes isn't supposed to indicate that I equate the terms, only that I would have identified as all 3), without having actually read Das Kapital or having the sufficient knowledge a person would probably need to call themselves a Marxist and be taken seriously.
	- I'm now of the view (as always, mutable) that the terms are ultimately too "loaded" with too many differing interpretations and with too little overlap between different minds' conceptions that I prefer to avoid them. Additionally, while I probably agree with many principles/ideas associated with the terms, I also probably disagree with some (I say "probably" because I don't have adequate knowledge to form any substantial agreement/disagreement for now) and so why would I position myself under the umbrella of a term with which I'm not 100% convinced about and confident in representing?
	- So, I prefer to just identify as nothing - if anything, just "someone who wants things to be better for everyone". Actually, I'd say the notion of The Objective has replaced any ideological identities - I would just consider myself / my efforts as oriented toward The Objective - the pursuit of the full freedom and flourishing of all, defined by each individual's subjective experience of it.

\* Some examples of how I used the brackets:
- **"Not \[everything](1) can be captured \[explained](2) holistically with data and numbers \[quantitative](3); these tools must be used in conjunction with \[qualitative analyses](4) to complement data, without abstracting away the essence of a subject of interest."**
	- Each instance of square brackets is numbered for reference below:
	- (1) I wanted a different, more specific word for "everything" than "everything". In this case, the \[word] acts as a **placeholder** (until I come up with a better one).
	- (2) I was weighing up the use of "captured" vs "explained" (and so appended the \[explained] to indicate that it was a possible replacement word for the initially used representing word "captured"). In this case, the \[word] acts as an **interchanger** (the words are interchangeable - though the meaning may be altered slightly, either fits with the existing sentence.
	- (3) I wanted to jog my brain in the direction of the term "quantitative" and other terms I might associate with it, and remind myself to try reworking the sentence to incorporate these (for example, replacing the preceding "data and numbers" with just "quantitative data"). I also probably sprinkled it in there because I thought "data and numbers" is a silly/woolly/imprecise thing to include (because e.g. not all "numbers" are considered "data", and not all "data" are considered "numbers") and so I wanted to flag this, so I would pay particular attention to eliminating the imprecision when editing. In this case, the \[word] acts as a **reminder** - to remind me to direct more thought into refining the stuff around it, taking into account whatever the reminder word/s is/are.
	- (4) Kind of a mixture of (1) and (3): I wanted to remind myself to clarify what "qualitative analyses" would even represent, as commonly conceived, and then consider whether a different term would be more appropriate.
- **"Biology can inform aspects of neuroscience, neuroscience is connected with psychology, and hence, biology is \[connected with] psychology."**
	- Here, I used the square brackets because I wanted to come up with something equivalent but different to "connected with" in my second use of the term.

So, the square brackets seem to serve as e.g. a placeholder, an interchanger, a reminder, or some mixture. I say "*seem* to serve" because I never really established any concrete rules/conventions for how I should use them - I just used them as I felt inclined to, and some usage patterns naturally emerged. Overall, I think they just represent uncertainty and/or lack of satisfaction with how I've expressed something, and remind me to pay particular attention to refining it when editing.
