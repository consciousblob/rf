Electrodynamics (or electromagnetism) is the study of phenomena associated with light. It studies 2 "types" of wave, which are thought to be "2 sides of the same coin" - electric, and magnetic. In fact, when relativity is accounted for (when we quantise the electromagnetic field ? and move into QED), it is possible to show that different observers (moving at some velocity relative to one another) can perceive the fields differently (e.g. see a magnetic field where the other sees an electric field).

Quantum mechanics is the non-relativistic (low speed/energy?) study of the smallest objects in existence.

QFT allows for the study of both relativistic and non-relativistic smallest-scale objects. This is because it treats particles as perturbations in a field, which represents all particles of that type ?
But then, is each particle represented by a different (its own) field, or is there just one field to "store" or "act on behalf of" all particles of that type? I think the latter, because we have the e.g. Higgs field, electron-positron field, electromagnetic field. And then, each individual particle is represented by a wave within that field?

QED is one instance of a QFT, specifically in the realm of electromagnetism (it is effectively the quantum version of classical electrodynamics, accounting for the phenomena/interactions of the smallest "representors" of light, photons).

Classical electrodynamics (EM): naturally relativistic, deterministic (studies light)
QM: non-relativistic (doesn't account for e.g. particle creation/annihilation at high energies ?), probabilistic/stochastic
QED: unifies both, accounting for wave nature of light in EM, but also particle interpretation of QM - it holds both the relativity of EM, and the stochasticity of QM.
