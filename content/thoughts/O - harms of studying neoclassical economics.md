From what I've observed and experienced, neoclassical economics is a mostly stagnant series of rather boring theoretical fiction, positioned as the scientific study of human nature and optimal economic arrangements.

I would sit down to draw my isocost curves to indicate the conditions of "perfect competition", and determine the "subsistence wage" an employee should receive to keep them sufficiently motivated while giving the *least* possible remuneration for their efforts, and rederive the arguments that yield the "natural rate of unemployment". But as I trudged through this content, I found it increasingly difficult to override the voice in my head screaming, "this is toxic bollocks you should not be exposing yourself to!" Now, my use of "toxic bollocks" may be perceived as at least mildly hyperbolic, so allow me to justify. Getting good grades for particularly theoretical modules (mainly, microeconomics and macroeconomics) requires a sort of internalisation of the theory - a temporary adoption of the taught worldview. But I found that the more intensely I tried to engage with the material, and see the world from its lens, the harder it became to recognise and cultivate my *own* worldview. My worldview was effectively being "contaminated" with frankly *unrealistic* conceptions of human nature and behaviour, that positions us as self-interested rational agents averse to doing *anything* good for anyone else unless we stand to gain from it.
